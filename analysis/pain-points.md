# Pain Point Analysis

## Overview

The current order management process relies on WhatsApp, a calendar, and pinned WhatsApp conversations. While these tools help the owner manage orders, several parts of the process depend on manual actions and memory.

The following pain points were identified from the current process and the observed missed-order incident.

## Identified Pain Points

### 1. Manual Order Recording

After an order is confirmed, the owner needs to manually record the order in the calendar.

This creates a dependency on the owner remembering to complete the recording step. If the order is not added to the calendar, it may not appear when the owner checks upcoming orders.

**Impact:**

* An upcoming order may be overlooked.
* Preparation planning can be affected.
* The owner may need to search through previous conversations to find the order.

---

### 2. Reliance on Pinned WhatsApp Chats

The owner uses pinned WhatsApp conversations to quickly access important customer orders.

However, pinning the conversation is also a manual action. If the conversation is not pinned, finding an older order may require searching through the customer's WhatsApp chat history.

**Impact:**

* Older orders can be difficult to locate.
* Additional time may be spent searching conversations.
* Important order information may not be immediately accessible.

---

### 3. Order Information Is Distributed Across Different Places

Information related to a single order is handled through multiple places.

For example:

* Customer requirements are stored in WhatsApp.
* The required date is recorded in the calendar.
* The WhatsApp conversation may be pinned separately.
* Payment information may be confirmed through the conversation or payment records.

There is no single structured view of the complete order.

**Impact:**

* The owner may need to check multiple sources.
* Information can become difficult to track.
* The process becomes dependent on keeping each source updated.

---

### 4. Dependence on Memory

Several important activities depend on the owner remembering to perform them.

After confirming an order, the owner needs to remember to:

1. Add the order to the calendar.
2. Pin the relevant WhatsApp conversation.
3. Check upcoming orders.
4. Prepare the cake according to the required date and time.
5. Confirm the remaining payment before handover.

If an important tracking action is missed, the rest of the process can be affected.

**Impact:**

* Increased risk of human error.
* Orders may not be identified at the expected time.
* Additional stress and manual checking may be required.

---

### 5. Difficulty Identifying an Order When Tracking Steps Are Missed

The observed incident demonstrated this issue.

A customer had placed an order approximately one month before the required date. However, the order was not added to the calendar and the WhatsApp conversation was not pinned.

The order was only identified when the customer asked whether a cake shown in a post was theirs. The owner then had to search through the previous WhatsApp conversation to confirm the order.

**Impact:**

* The order was discovered only one day before it was required.
* The owner had limited time to respond and prepare.
* The situation created uncertainty for both the owner and customer.

---

### 6. Risk to Preparation Planning

Cake preparation depends on the required date and time.

Morning orders may need to be prepared on the previous day, while later orders may require preparation early on the required date.

If an upcoming order is not identified in time, the owner may not have sufficient preparation time.

**Impact:**

* Reduced preparation time.
* Increased pressure on the owner.
* Potential delays in completing the order.
* Increased risk of affecting cake quality or delivery/pickup timing.

---

### 7. Customer Confidence Risk

Customers expect their confirmed orders to be remembered and prepared for the agreed date.

When the owner is unable to immediately confirm whether an order exists, it can create uncertainty for the customer.

Although the observed incident did not result in a confirmed missed delivery, the situation could negatively affect customer confidence if similar incidents occur repeatedly.

**Impact:**

* Reduced customer confidence.
* Potential damage to the business's reputation.
* Possible loss of repeat customers.

---

## Pain Point Summary

| ----- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| PP-01 | Manual order recording                         | Calendar entry must be created manually                 | Order may not appear in upcoming schedule   |
| PP-02 | Reliance on pinned chats                       | Chat must be manually pinned                            | Older conversations become harder to access |
| PP-03 | Information distributed across multiple places | WhatsApp, calendar and pinned chats are used separately | Information is harder to track              |
| PP-04 | Dependence on owner's memory                   | Several tracking activities are manual                  | Increased risk of human error               |
| PP-05 | Difficult order retrieval                      | Order may remain inside an old WhatsApp conversation    | Time is spent searching for order details   |
| PP-06 | Preparation planning risk                      | Preparation depends on identifying upcoming orders      | Reduced preparation time or possible delays |
| PP-07 | Customer confidence risk                       | Owner may be unable to immediately confirm an order     | Reduced customer confidence                 |

## Key Finding

The main weakness identified in the current process is the **dependence on manual actions to ensure that confirmed orders are transferred from WhatsApp into the owner's tracking methods**.

The observed missed-order incident demonstrates the consequence of this dependency. When the calendar entry and WhatsApp pin were missed, the order became difficult to identify until the customer brought it to the owner's attention.

The next stage of the analysis will investigate the underlying causes of these pain points rather than treating the missed order itself as the root problem.
